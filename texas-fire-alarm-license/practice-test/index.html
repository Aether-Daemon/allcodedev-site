Or take one exam at a time

This is the single biggest preparation mistake: the two exams share a licence and share almost no content. TFM11 is a statute exam with no code in it. TFM12 is a code exam with almost no statute in it. Studying them as one pile wastes most of the effort.

  • TFM11 practice test — Texas Insurance Code Chapter 6002 and 28 TAC 34.600. Twenty questions.
  • TFM12 practice test — NFPA 72, NEC Article 760, circuits and calculations. Twenty questions.

How these questions were built

Three things worth knowing, because they are the things most free question sets get wrong:

  • The edition is pinned. Texas adopted NFPA 72-2019 through 28 TAC §34.607 in March 2023. Every section number printed here was checked against the adopted edition; where a number could not be confirmed identical in that edition, the citation is given at chapter level rather than invented. A renumbered section is worse than no citation.
  • The exam facts come from the SFMO. 50 questions, 70% to pass, PSI, closed book. Fire alarm licensing in Texas is the State Fire Marshal’s Office, not TDLR — and sites that say TDLR tend to get the question count wrong too.
  • Explanations say why. A practice question that only tells you the letter teaches you nothing you can use on a different question about the same rule. Roughly half the technical-exam answers also name the section, so you can go and read it.

These are not the real exam. They are independently written practice questions covering the same material. Nobody selling you practice questions has the actual bank, and anyone implying otherwise is lying to you.
